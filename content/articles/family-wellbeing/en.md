Modern technology has replaced many things that used to be essential at home.

"The family is the basic institution of society, the natural setting for the growth of its members and a universal phenomenon; that is why, in this sense, it is a good thing to encourage family activities as a way to strengthen family relationships."

Nurturing these activities will help new generations have more freedom and less violence, more security and less chaos, more sense of home and not the emptiness of an indifferent environment.

The importance of the family and its well-being helps build a society that spreads and supports bonds of fellowship, mutual respect and a happy life together.

## Some ideas to boost family well-being

Build positive family habits and a lifestyle that supports good health. Look at the following factors and see what changes you can make to boost your family's well-being.

1. **Nutrition:** We all know we should cut back on fats and sugars and eat more fruits, vegetables, grains, fiber, vitamins and minerals. Focus on the foods your family eats every day and make changes gradually.
2. **Exercise and movement:** Joining a gym would be ideal, but the reality is that our many commitments often make that impossible or very hard. Drive less and walk more, take a family walk once a week, buy toys that encourage active play (fewer PlayStation games and more hula hoops, limbo, Twister and so on) and visit your nearest playground more often.
3. **More time outdoors, more physical activity and less TV, video games and phones:** Cut down the time your family spends in front of a screen and spend more time outside.
4. **Communication and quality family time:** A family that spends time together and talks is a close family. Share one meal a day with your kids: sharing dinner as a family has been linked with less depression in teens, and it will surely help you know what your kids are up to and whether they are having a problem. Kids learn many things at school, but values and self-esteem come from home. Turn down the radio, put your phone away and ask your kids how their day went; work conversations into your routine, like on the drive from home to school.
