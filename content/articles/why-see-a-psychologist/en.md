## Why do people see a psychologist?

Just as we often go to a physical therapist for a muscle problem and think of it as one more visit to a health professional, today many people see a psychologist to take care of their mental health.

Even so, there are still people who, because they don't have a clear idea of what a psychologist does, assume that anyone who goes to a psychology appointment must be vulnerable, weak or "crazy," and that idea is completely wrong.

What's more, this kind of thinking only makes people feel insecure or ashamed when someone suggests they see a psychologist, so they end up hiding it or putting it off, and as a result, they don't get to solve their problems.

Thanks to how society has moved forward and how content spreads on social media, today far more people understand the benefits of seeing a mental health professional than hold on to old beliefs.

## What does a psychologist do in therapy?

To get into the main topic of this post and clear up a few questions, we can say that psychology professionals help people understand and change their thoughts, as well as change behaviors and the ways they relate to others. And although a patient may come in for a "current" problem, the professional will use a range of tools to help the patient face situations that have happened throughout their life. In fact, many traumas or situations people go through as adults stem from past causes hidden in the unconscious, and the professional is the one who helps release that distress.

There are countless reasons people may see a psychologist: family problems, anger, depression, panic attacks, difficulty fitting in socially, anxiety, fears, and the list could go on forever.

Each patient will need a different kind of therapy to help them resolve their problem. That's why each professional specializes in a different area, using a variety of techniques and tools to explore the issue in depth and help the patient heal. Often, people need to express their problems and go through a process of mental and spiritual healing, and they find calm with a professional who guides them through specific meditations.

In other cases, the psychologist will need help from other professionals, such as psychiatrists, who are in charge of prescribing the right medication and following up with the patient.
